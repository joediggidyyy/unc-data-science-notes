<td colspan="2"><p>A quick search on Google Scholar reveals a large volume of papers on the effect of AI on human resource efforts in the healthcare industry. Randomly selecting three articles from the first page of results, only one of the papers offered any meaningful discussion of the ethical implications or bias risks. This was not the main focus of the paper, though at least it was given fair analysis.</p>
<p>My first thought was to check the publication dates: all were from 2023. I repeated the random selection, but this time I filtered for 2025 and 2026 publications only. As I suspected, this time all three mentioned ethical concerns, with one paper specifically focused on the ethical quandaries and another devoting a substantial portion of its research to issues including bias and displacement.</p>
<p>This suggests, at least circumstantially, that concern about the ethical implications of AI on the workforce and hiring practices is increasing. This is not surprising—given the rapid and accelerating advancement of agentic AI—that in only two years, public discussion of these emerging technologies has become more grounded in real-life implications and less focused on theory.</p>
